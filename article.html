Fed signals first rate cut of 2026 as inflation cools to 2.4 percent target band

Chair Jerome Powell indicated the FOMC is "increasingly confident" inflation is moving sustainably toward target, opening the door to a March cut that would reshape currency markets and Treasury yields globally.

SC
Sarah Chen
Senior Markets Correspondent
Published Sep 14, 2026 · 14:00 UTC
Updated 14:30 UTC
7 min read

The Federal Reserve's Eccles Building in Washington, D.C. Photo: FxTopics editorial

The Federal Reserve is preparing to deliver its first interest rate cut of 2026 in March, according to comments made by Chair Jerome Powell during a press conference following the conclusion of the Federal Open Market Committee's two-day policy meeting on Wednesday.

Powell described the recent inflation data as "the most encouraging sequence of prints we've seen since the tightening cycle began," with the core personal consumption expenditures index falling to 2.4 percent year-on-year — within the central bank's stated tolerance band around its 2 percent target.

Market implications across asset classes

The prospect of lower policy rates has already begun reshaping positioning across global financial markets. The dollar index has weakened 2.1 percent since the start of the month, while the yield on the benchmark 10-year Treasury note has compressed by 18 basis points to 3.84 percent.

Equity markets have responded favorably to the dovish tilt, with the S&P 500 reaching a new all-time high in the immediate aftermath of Powell's comments. Rate-sensitive sectors including housing, utilities and small-cap equities have led the advance.

"We are increasingly confident that inflation is moving sustainably toward our 2 percent objective. If the data continues to evolve as expected, it will be appropriate to begin adjusting the stance of policy." — Jerome Powell, FOMC Chair

What traders are watching next

Markets are now focused on three key data releases that will shape the FOMC's calculus at its March meeting: the January employment report, the December core PCE deflator, and the upcoming fourth-quarter earnings season which will provide fresh insight into corporate pricing power.

According to the CME FedWatch tool, futures traders are now pricing in a 78 percent probability of a 25 basis point reduction at the March meeting, up from 54 percent one week ago. The probability of a second cut by June has risen to 42 percent.

Currency market positioning

The dollar's weakness has been particularly pronounced against the yen and the euro. USD/JPY has fallen below the psychologically important 150 level that had previously attracted intervention rhetoric from Japanese officials. EUR/USD has broken above 1.0850, with traders now eyeing resistance at 1.0950.

  • DXY index down 2.1 percent month-to-date
  • 10-year Treasury yield at 3.84 percent, down 18 bps
  • S&P 500 reaches new all-time high
  • Gold up 4.2 percent in September to $2,684
  • Bitcoin reclaims $67,000 amid broader risk-on move

The broader policy context

The Fed's pivot comes amid a synchronized easing cycle across major developed market central banks. The European Central Bank cut rates by 25 basis points earlier this month, while the Bank of England is widely expected to follow suit at its February meeting. The Bank of Japan remains the principal outlier, having just concluded its first policy tightening in 17 years.

Analysts at major investment banks have begun revising their forecasts. Goldman Sachs now expects three cuts of 25 basis points each in 2026, while JPMorgan's economics team has moved to a forecast of two cuts, arguing that the Fed will want to preserve optionality given lingering services inflation pressures.

For currency traders, the path forward will depend heavily on the relative pace of easing. A more aggressive Fed combined with a cautious ECB could limit euro upside despite the immediate reaction, while a steady BoJ could keep yen volatility elevated as the rate differential continues to compress.

The next major data point will be Friday's retail sales report, which will provide the first comprehensive read on consumer spending patterns heading into the year-end holiday period.
